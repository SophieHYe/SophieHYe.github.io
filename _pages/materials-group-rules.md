## Rule 1 — Meetings

Meeting time with your supervisors is a limited resource — please respect it and make
full use of it.

That means **coming prepared, with slides ready in advance**. Do not use the meeting
itself to work out what you want to say. Prepare the deck following the
[weekly report template](/materials/weekly-report/), and we will discuss from it.

**No last-minute cancellations.** Having no update is not a reason to cancel — come to
the meeting anyway and we will talk about why the week stalled. If you genuinely cannot
attend, tell **everyone invited** at least **one day in advance**. Illness is the only
exception.

## Rule 2 — Be in the lab

Come to the lab **at least twice a week**. Research is not a solo activity: being
physically around your colleagues is how you pick up what others are working on, get
unstuck quickly, and have the conversations that never happen over chat.

## Rule 3 — Get API budget approved before you run

Before spending anything on API credits, send me a short **budget proposal** covering:

- **which models** you need, and **how much budget** each one takes;
- **which experiments** you are running, stated concretely;
- whether these are **final experiments** or still exploratory.

Wait for approval before launching the runs. No approval, no spend.

## Rule 4 — Give a talk every three months

I care a great deal about students learning to **present their work**. It is a skill
that has to be practised, and it should not wait until you have a paper to present.

So: **every three months, give a talk** to the group on where your research stands.
A talk without a paper behind it is perfectly fine — a progress update, a half-formed
idea, or a negative result all count.
